**Q: Why the centered difference and not `(f(x+h) − f(x)) / h`?**

The one-sided version is off by something proportional to `h`. For the circle it's exactly `πh`, and for `x²`
it's `xh`. The centered version cancels that term and leaves something proportional to `h²`. I drew this
before writing any code (the ring and sliver animations in the README), and it made the formula feel obvious
rather than memorized.

**Q: Does my softmax + cross-entropy gradient actually match?**

Yes. The max relative error over all 12 entries of `dL/dW` was `1.35e-8`, and the mean was `7.5e-9`.

**Q: Then why won't it go lower than 1e-8, no matter what h I pick?**

This was the best surprise of the day. The h sweep has a flat floor at about 1.4e-8 between `h = 1e-4` and
`1e-6`, and round-off doesn't explain a *flat* floor. The cause is the `+ 1e-9` inside `log(probs + 1e-9)`.
The numerical gradient differentiates the loss I actually wrote, including that 1e-9. The analytical formula
`probs − y` assumes the plain `log`. Taking the 1e-9 out drops the error to `3.2e-10`.

So a gradient check doesn't compare my formula to the "true" derivative. It compares my formula to my code.
Usually that's what I want, but it means tiny tweaks like an epsilon show up in the number.

**Q: How much does h matter?**

A lot, in both directions. At `h = 1e-1` the error is ~4e-3, which counts as a "bug" on the CS231n scale even
though nothing is wrong. At `h = 1e-12` it's back up to ~4e-3 from round-off. A failed gradient check with a
bad `h` would have sent me hunting for a bug that doesn't exist.

**Q: How much faster is NumPy, really?**

| n | speedup |
|---:|---:|
| 100 | ~45x |
| 1,000 | ~290x |
| 10,000 | ~510x |
| 100,000 | ~600x |
| 1,000,000 | ~200x |

I expected it to keep climbing toward 1000x. It peaked at 100k and fell back at 1M. The loop costs about 140 ns
per element at every size. NumPy is the one that changes: from 100k to 1M its time grew about 30x for 10x the
data. My guess is that 3 arrays × 8 MB no longer fit in cache, so it's waiting on memory instead of computing.
I haven't tested that.

## Bugs I hit, short version

1. `np.arange(1, 5)` is int. `x[i] += 1e-5` truncates, so `x − h` became `x − 1` and the "gradient" came out
   50,000x too big. Fixed with `x.astype(float)`, plus a test.
2. `keedims=True`. NumPy's error message (`unexpected keyword argument`) was clear, at least.
3. `np.eye(4)[:6]` gives 4 rows, not 6. Slicing past the end doesn't add rows. `np.eye(C)[labels]` does what
   I meant.
4. `__all__` listed `analytical_grad_W`, which doesn't exist. Nothing broke because I never used `import *`,
   so it would have stayed hidden.
